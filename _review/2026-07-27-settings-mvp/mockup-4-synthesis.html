Why this is the recommended synthesis

Target user: 25–55 NZ tramping community. Not a novice, not a hacker — a grown-up who wants their app to work and get out of the way.

Language: grown-up, not childish

Section names are plain English adult vocabulary: Preferences, Memory, About & Legal, Account. No "Your app", no "Your walks", no "Distance & height". A 45-year-old tramper reading "Distance & height" instead of "Units" will feel talked-down-to — this is the demographic that reads MetService discussion forums and DOC route notes.

vs Mockup 1 (Novice) Kept the warm cream palette and card grouping. Dropped the "Your app" / "Your walks" / "Distance & height" relabels — those cross the line from friendly into patronising for the actual target user.

Icons: only where they help scanning

Rule applied: category rows get an icon, verb rows don't. "Units", "Night mode", "Privacy Policy" — these are categories, a tile helps you locate them quickly. "Change password", "Clear all memory", "Sign out", "Delete account" — these are verbs you already read; adding an icon is decoration and adds visual noise.

vs Mockup 3 (Minimal) Kept the whitespace discipline and rejection of decorative icons on action rows. Added back category icons because with 6 rows in About & Legal, pure text becomes a scanning burden for a user on a rainy trailhead with cold hands.

Sub-captions: only when they carry real info

The one sub-caption in the whole screen is on Delete account: "7-day cool-off period · you can cancel" — that's genuinely useful because it reduces fear-of-clicking and it's true. No fabricated "saves ~18% battery" captions, no marketing prose.

Version metadata sits inline on About Cairn as v0.2.5 · O11 — real data, correct location (right side value slot), also serving as the 5-tap Developer unlock target.

vs Mockup 2 (Power) Kept the precise version string and the walled-off danger zone with a visible red top-border. Dropped the fake performance sub-captions ("saves ~18% battery", "reduces GPS drift") — these are unverified promises and violate Cairn's "no fake promises" rule.

Memory: one line, real data only

"47 places · 3 planted" — no km², no elevation gained, no streaks. If the data store actually contains it today it goes in. If not, it stays out. The row is readonly (no chevron), signalling it's information not a control.

vs Mockup 3 (Minimal) Dropped the prose "You've collected 47 places over 12 walks..." — reads like a magazine and hides the two operational rows (view / clear) below it. Data as data, not narrative.

Danger zone: physically and visually separated

Sign out and Delete account are in different sections — grey Account card first, then a distinct Delete Account card with a 2px red top-border. A tired user tapping "Sign out" cannot mis-hit "Delete account" — they're not even in the same visual container.

The Delete row uses a red accent (border + red text label) but not a full red button. A big red button reads as "primary destructive action available" — we want the opposite: "only touch this if you really mean it."

Developer section: revealed, not hidden crudely

5-tap on the About Cairn row unlocks a Developer section at the bottom (shown here in its unlocked state). Chosen over "DEBUG" because "Developer" reads professional — matches how Apple's own Settings > Developer works.

Visual treatment differs from normal cards: warm off-white fill, dashed border, small "Unlocked" badge in the section header, and a section caption "Only for development and QA." — so it's clearly a different mode, not a shipping feature the user just discovered.

Brand warmth: kept exactly one thing

"Ngā mihi nui — thanks for using Cairn." — one italic footer line, no emoji, no exclamation. This is the NZ touch that the target user recognises without feeling pandered to. It's genuine te reo, it's brief, and it's already in the codebase.
