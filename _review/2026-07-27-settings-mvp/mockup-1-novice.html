Design rationale — Novice lens

Every decision below traces back to Sarah: 35, office worker, three walks under her belt, opens Settings when something confuses her.
"I just want to change one thing. I don't want to feel like I'm going to break something. And please don't make me read a wall of text."

1. Section labels rewritten in plain English

The IA draft used engineering labels (Preferences, Memory, About & Legal, Danger zone). Sarah doesn't parse "Preferences" — she scans for the thing she needs. So:

  • Preferences → Your app (things about how the app behaves)
  • Memory → Your walks (Sarah calls them walks, not "memory")
  • About & Legal → Help & info (help comes first — she's looking for help before she's looking for legal)
  • Danger zone → Account (the word "danger" scares her; grouping under "Account" is the standard iOS pattern she already knows from Apple ID)

2. Row-level hints under every meaningful label

Sarah opens Settings, sees "Haptic feedback", and thinks: what does that even mean? Under each row I added a one-line hint in the same warm voice: "Little buzz when you tap buttons." She never has to guess.

3. Order flipped — help before legal

In the IA draft, Privacy Policy comes first. But Sarah opens Settings for help 10x more often than for legal. I put Weather / Safety / Feedback at the top of "Help & info", then Privacy / Terms / About below. Legal is still discoverable — it's just not first.

4. MetService weather framed as advice, not a link

"Check the weather — always check before you head out" is written like a trusted friend tells her. This is a NZ-specific safety norm (DOC guidance). It builds Cairn's credibility as an outdoor app that actually cares.

5. Destructive actions buried, colours muted

"Clear my walk history" uses a soft warm-red icon background (#f5e8e6 / #b25a48) — not the aggressive iOS red (#ff3b30). It's still discoverable but doesn't scream. "Delete my account" gets slightly more saturated red text (#c44545) because it deserves gravity, but the chevron is a pale pink so the row doesn't visually shout across the whole page.

Sarah's biggest fear is "did I just delete everything?" — so both destructive rows have hints promising a confirmation step or 7-day recovery window.

6. Sign out has a reassuring hint

"You can sign back in anytime — your walks stay saved." Novices sometimes think signing out will lose their data. One sentence solves it.

7. Identity card at top — one-glance identity check

Letter avatar (S) in Cairn green, name, email, then a subtle "Change password" link under a divider. Sarah confirms she's in the right account before making changes. SSO users won't see the Change password link.

8. Icon palette — warm, muted, no warnings

Every icon uses a soft tinted background matching Cairn's palette (moss green, warm cream, dusty blue, soft rust). Zero use of stark iOS system colours. The destructive icons use the same muted rust as trail markers, not a fire-engine red.

9. Debug hidden behind a hint, not a mystery

A tiny italic line above the version tells Sarah what tapping 5x does — so if she accidentally triggers it, she isn't scared. Discoverable but non-intrusive.

10. What I explicitly did NOT do

  • No sub-section subheaders inside cards (Sarah scans the whole card as one unit)
  • No "advanced" or "developer" jargon anywhere visible
  • No red "Delete" filled buttons — only text colour changes
  • No overwhelming section per screen — 4 cards fit her mental model of "what kind of thing"

Open questions for you

  • Is "Your walks" too casual? Alternative: "My walks" or "Walk history"
  • Should the version string include the OTA build (2026.10.15) at launch, or hide until debug is unlocked?
  • Should the identity card show the current subscription tier (Founder / Free / Paid) for the paid tier launch, or wait until post-MVP?
