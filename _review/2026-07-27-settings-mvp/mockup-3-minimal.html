Minimalist perspective

Linear, Arc, and Notion taught us that a Settings page is not a control panel — it's a reading experience. Users open it maybe once a week. When they do, they should feel like they're reading a well-designed magazine page, not wrestling with a dashboard. Every card, colored track, and decorative icon the iOS default gives you for free is a small demand on attention. Removed.

Design rationale

"Each row is a sentence. Each section is a paragraph."

What was removed, and why

  • Section cards. The rounded-white iOS grouped-list card is visual chrome that carries no information. Whitespace and a single hairline rule between sections do the same grouping work, silently.
  • Row icons. The 📏 🎨 📳 emoji column that the IA draft showed is decorative. "Units" is already the word for units — an icon of a ruler beside the word "Units" is redundant. Icons were kept only where they add signal: the ↗ glyph on external links (MetService, Report safety) actually tells the user "you will leave the app."
  • Colored toggle tracks. The iOS-default green switch screams for attention. A greyscale track that turns matte-black when on is just as legible and stops competing with the mountain-green brand.
  • Bold section headers. Replaced with 10 px letterspaced small-caps in a soft grey. They classify the region without shouting.
  • Destructive red backgrounds. The whole page has one visible danger color, and it appears only on hover of "Clear all my memory" and "Delete account." Restraint makes the red actually mean something when it does show up.

What was regrouped

  • Sign out is with Account, not Danger. Signing out is a routine act. Deleting an account is a permanent one. Putting them in the same "Danger zone" trains users to click through warnings — the exact muscle memory you don't want when they reach Delete.
  • Memory reads as prose. "You've collected 47 places and have 3 cairns left to plant this month" is warmer, more accurate, and takes less vertical space than a stats row followed by a bin icon.
  • The version footer is the debug door. Centered, muted, unclickable-looking — five taps unlock Debug. No visible affordance because no ordinary user should find it.

What was kept from iOS convention

  • The 393 × 852 canvas, the ‹ Back affordance, and the right-aligned toggle pattern. Convention is only worth breaking when the break yields something. A vertical toggle would gain nothing.
  • The Change-password link inside the identity card. It's the one account action email users need often enough to surface, but not so often it earns a section of its own.

What this design bets on

That Cairn's users — NZ hikers, editorial-minded, outdoor-warm — reward restraint. The warm off-white background (#faf7f2), the serif letter avatar, and the italic Iowan pull quote in this rationale are the only expressive moments. Everything else steps back so the content can speak. If a user opens Settings and thinks nothing about the design, we won.
